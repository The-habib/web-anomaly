## 1. Prior Art Taxonomy

The phenomenon of long-surviving web artifacts and unmodernized HTML infrastructure is documented across multiple academic and archiving disciplines:

1. **Web Archival & Link Rot Studies**:
   - Koehler (2004) — *A longitudinal study of Web pages continued: a consideration of document persistence*.
   - Ainsworth et al. (2011) — *How much of the Web is archived?* (Old Dominion University / Mementoweb).
2. **Web Design Archaeology**:
   - Olia Lialina & Dragan Espenschied (2009) — *Digital Folklore: To Computer Users, with Love and Respect* (Geocities / Retro layout documentation).
   - Jason Scott (2000–present) — *Textfiles.com* and Internet Archive software preservation.
3. **Automated Anomaly Detection on Web Graphs**:
   - Klein et al. (2014) — *Detecting structural and semantic drift in longitudinal web archives*.

---

## 2. Atlas Scientific Contribution

Project Atlas does not claim that legacy web pages were never observed before. Rather, Project Atlas contributes:
- A **formal evidence-first anomaly validation pipeline** with zero synthetic tolerance.
- A **cryptographically verifiable provenance ledger** tying every claim directly to frozen HTTP and CDX artifacts.
- A **blind, score-hiding human review protocol** designed to prevent confirmation bias in computational web archaeology.

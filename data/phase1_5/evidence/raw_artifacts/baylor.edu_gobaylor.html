    Early Decision Benefits

    Students admitted through Early Decision, who meet all application and deposit deadlines, will receive: 

    • First Choice Housing Preference:
      New first-year students who submit a completed application by November 1 as an Early Decision applicant (Binding), submit their enrollment deposit by the Early Decision Deposit Deadline, and apply for housing by the Housing Priority Deadline (April 15) will receive:
      • First choice of housing, space permitting (Living-Learning Communities and Residential Colleges require meeting minimum program requirements)
      • A "Choose Your Own Room" time slot assigned ahead of the general incoming class, based on your deposit dates
      • The opportunity to participate in Roommate Matching early
    • Priority Academic Advising Registration:
      Get early access to schedule your academic advising appointment before the general incoming student population. A student who selects an earlier appointment time will have the opportunity to plan their schedule sooner, which may provide access to a wider selection of available courses.
    • $3,000 Non-Renewable Scholarship:
      Receive a one-time scholarship in recognition of your Early Decision commitment to Baylor.

    Post-Baccalaureate Application

    Students should apply as a post-baccalaureate applicant if they:

    • Have already earned a bachelor's degree from an accredited institution
    • Are seeking a different degree or additional coursework with a different major from their first degree

    To apply via the Baylor Web App, make sure you have first created an account above.

    • Applicants who have not previously applied to Baylor should submit an application for admission.
      • On the application, select Post-Baccalaureate as your student type.
    • Students who previously applied to Baylor should submit a new application for admission.
    • All applicants should submit a transcript to Undergraduate Admissions showing the degree already earned.
    • After being accepted, submit a Health Form with documentation of bacterial meningitis vaccination.

    Applications received after the deadline will be reviewed on a space-available basis.

    Non-Degree Application

    Students should apply as a non-degree applicant if they:

    • Have NOT already earned a bachelor's degree from an accredited institution
    • Are NOT intending to pursue a degree from Baylor
    • Are seeking to take classes for personal enrichment for one of the following reasons:

    An exchange student is one who is pursuing a degree at another school and will be participating in one of Baylor's exchange programs.These students apply for admission with the help of the exchange program director.

    Questions may be directed to International Student and Scholars Services at ISSS_Support@baylor.edu. Learn more about International Student and Scholar Services.

    Students desiring to participate in one of Baylor's study abroad programs but are pursuing a degree at another college or university may apply as a non-degree seeking student.

    Students should submit an application for admission and write the name of the study abroad program they plan to participate in.

    Questions may be directed to Study Abroad at Study_Abroad_Interest@baylor.edu. Learn more about Study Abroad.

    Students currently attending another university who want to take Baylor classes and have these credits transferred to their current school.

    • Students should submit a new application for admission and a current transcript or letter of good standing from their current college or university to Undergraduate Admissions.
    • On the application, select Non-Degree Seeking/Visiting as your student type. There is no need to select an intended program or major.
    • After being accepted, submit a Health Form with documentation of bacterial meningitis vaccination and review the Registration Checklist.

    Other students who wish to take non-degree coursework at Baylor and are not currently attending a college or university should:

    • Submit an application for admission. Additional steps might be required based on individual circumstances.
    • After being accepted, submit a Health Form with documentation of bacterial meningitis vaccination.

    To apply via the Baylor Web App, make sure you have first created an account above.

    Applications received after the deadline will be reviewed on a space-available basis.

    Accelerate Application

    Students should apply as an Accelerate applicant if they:

    • Are currently a Junior or Senior in high school
      • (i.e. Current Sophomores may apply for the summer after Sophomore year)
    • Are interested in getting ahead on course work in pursuit of a Bachelor's degree

    To apply via the Baylor Web App, make sure you have first created an account.

    Baylor University's Accelerate Pre-College Program gives high school students the opportunity to begin their academic pursuits and get ahead on core course requirements that will apply to a range of Baylor degrees, but are also transferable to other universities.

    This is a unique opportunity to get a jump start that will prepare students for college-level rigor. Learn more about Accelerate.

    • Talk to your high school counselor about your interest and eligibility.
    • Submit an application for admission and choose Undergraduate Student. Select "Start New Application," then "Accelerate" and apply for admission to Baylor.
    • Students who participate in the program continuously through the fall and spring semesters, or do not attend only the summer semester, need not reapply.
      • Students who do not attend a fall or spring semester will need to submit a new application upon returning to the program.

    Applications received after the deadline will be reviewed on a space-available basis.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Start off on the right foot with answers to some of the common questions about applying to Baylor!

    No, an interview is not required as part of the Baylor admission process.

    Yes. Students are not required to sign any statement of faith to be accepted to or to attend Baylor. In fact, our students come from 50 U.S. states and 100+ foreign countries, and that diversity means a variety of backgrounds, cultures and beliefs.

    No, we do not select students based on their religious affiliation or legacy status. We do ask these questions on the application in order to provide additional data to add to your profile.

    Yes, you can change your major. Just contact our office, and we can update that for you. At Baylor, when admitted to the university, you aren't admitted to a particular school or college.

    This gives you the flexibility to change majors at any time, contingent on meeting any departmental requirements prior to or upon enrolling.

    Baylor offers online courses as part of the Accelerate programs.

    As a private institution, Baylor's costs are the same regardless of your residency status.

    Nationality does not affect the cost of attending Baylor since Baylor is a private institution.

    Yes, students are allowed to have cars on campus. Vehicles must be registered with Parking Services. Parking permits are available for purchase for the year, semester or summer session.

    Baylor is a medium-size campus, and students can easily walk from one side of the campus to the other in about 15 minutes.

    Aside from walking or biking, Baylor provides shuttle services around campus.

    The Baylor University Police Department is committed to ensuring the safest campus possible.

    Most crimes reported to Baylor police involve property crimes. This includes thefts, burglaries and criminal mischiefs. Crimes against persons such as assaults, robberies and sexual offenses are historically low.

    The Office of Access and Learning Accommodation is committed to ensuring equal access in all aspects of the college experience for students with disabilities in all of Baylor’s programs, including those taking courses online.

    Accommodations can include any changes, modifications, equipment, or services requested by students because of their disability—that includes classroom, housing, meal plan or mobility needs.

    No. Admitted students are automatically considered for merit-based scholarships.

    In addition to merit scholarships, Baylor offers some scholarships for students who demonstrate financial need.

    Be sure to complete your financial aid application(s) to see if you qualify for institutional or federal need-based scholarships:

    U.S. Citizens, Permanent Residents or Eligible Non-Citizens:

    • Spring/Summer 2027: Submit BOTH the 2026-27 FAFSA & CSS Profile
    • Fall 2027: Submit ONLY the 2027-28 FAFSA

    International Students

    • Spring/Summer 2027: Submit the  2026-27 CSS Profile
    • Fall 2027: Submit the Baylor International Financial Aid Application (BIFAA)

    Departmental scholarships are also available and while they are generally awarded to upperclassmen, a few departments have scholarships for incoming freshmen.

    Outside scholarships can also be found online, and we encourage you to apply for those. Your high school counselor should also be able to assist you in determining what is available in your community.

    Learn more about the variety of scholarships available here.

    There are a variety of free scholarship search sites online. Here are just a few of the sites you should check out:

    • fastweb.com
    • fscholarships.com
    • collegenet.com
    • collegescholarships.org

    Baylor is a welcoming place and is home to students from many different faith traditions from all over the world. This diversity enriches our culture. With 400+ student organizations on campus, there are opportunities for everyone to get immersed in the Baylor culture, regardless of your background.

    You can even start a new organization if you're passionate about something that's not already represented. If you're interested in a religious organization that is not Christian, there are local places of worship that often meet students' needs.

    There are also vast opportunities for student employment on campus. Some paid student leadership positions have a Christian requirement, but the majority of student positions are open to all religions.

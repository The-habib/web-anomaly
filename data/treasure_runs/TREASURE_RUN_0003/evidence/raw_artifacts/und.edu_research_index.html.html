UND is a Carnegie Research 1 (R1) University

Carnagie R1...what does this mean?

The University of North Dakota is classified as a Carnegie R1: Very High Research Activity university - a designation shared with only 187 universities nationwide and representing about 4.7% of the nearly 4,000 degree-granting institutions in the nation. With an international reputation for research in health sciences, nutrition, energy and environmental protection, aerospace and engineering, going to a top-tier research university like UND is unique.

Students are an Integral Part of Research

Studies show students who engage in research are twice as likely to graduate, five-times more likely to go on to graduate school, and have more successful careers after graduation. The 516-acre campus in Grand Forks, North Dakota, provides a dynamic setting where discovery, teaching, and service thrive with a welcoming college community of more than 70,000.

On-Campus Research Units

Behavioral Health

The Behavioral Health Initiative focuses on behavioral health research, training, outreach, and service in North Dakota and beyond.

Biomedical

Responsible for the care of animals used in research, the Center for Biomedical Research (CBR) is state-of-the-art animal research facility.

Energy and Environment

The Energy & Environmental Research Center (EERC) is a national leader, providing pioneering solutions to the world's challenges.

Engineering

UND’s College of Engineering & Mines has several research centers and initiatives focused on engineering, computer science and geology.

Human Nutrition

One of six centers operated by the USDA, the Human Nutrition Research Center is a world leader in nutrition research for more than 30 years.

National Security

This initiative enhances national security through cutting-edge research and education of the next generation of experts, driving economic development and supporting a thriving society.

Rural Health

The Center for Rural Health supports rural and tribal communities, examining health disparities, delivery and systems, workforce and population health.

STEM

The North Dakota Established Program to Stimulate Competitive Research (ND EPSCoR) supports research in science, technology, engineering and mathematics.

Unmanned Aircraft Systems

The Research Institute for Autonomous Systems (RIAS) connects unmanned and autonomous research with economic development.

Computational Research Center

The Computational Research Center (CRC) is dedicated to helping University of North Dakota researchers solve challenging problems in science and society. 

Chester Fritz Library

UND's main library and the state's largest. The Chester Fritz holds over 1.6 million volumes, plus thousands of online resources.

The library includes renovated study space, group work rooms, and a Special Collections department that documents regional history and genealogical resources.

Visit the Fritz
SMHS Library Resources

Access to health sciences information for UND's main campus and other clinical campuses across the state.

The library also provides health information access training and services throughout the state.

MEDICAL and Health Sciences LIBRARY
Thormodsgard Law Library

More than 321,000 volumes, a growing audio-visual collection, and a variety of legal databases.

The collection reflects the Core Collection as set forth by the American Bar Association.

Law Library
Gordon Erickson Music Library

The most extensive music library in North Dakota and northwestern Minnesota.

Over 8,000 LP records, 6,600 CDs, 800 tapes and thousands of pieces of sheet music from all styles and periods.

Music Library
EERC Library

Highly specialized to serve the needs of the Energy & Environmental Research Center.

Its collection, also available to the wider campus community, is focused on the EERC’s current and historical core research areas.

Energy Library
F. D. Holland Geology Library

The largest geoscience library in the upper Midwest located in Leonard Hall.

Subjects include geology, environmental geology, hydrogeology, paleontology, geological engineering, mining, petroleum geology and North Dakota geology.

Geology Library
Wilson M. Laird Core and Sample Library

Houses 80% of the state's core samples, including 95% of the samples from the Williston basin.

The facility has 39,000 square feet of warehouse space and 12,000 square feet of laboratories and office space.

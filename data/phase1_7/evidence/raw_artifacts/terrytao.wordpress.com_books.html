Books

NOTE: these pages are no longer maintained.  The updated version of these pages can be found here.

In these pages are the latest information (including sample chapters and errata) for all the various books that I have been an author of:

  1. T. Tao, Solving mathematical problems: a personal perspective, Deakin University Press, 1992.
  2. T. Tao, Three regularity results in harmonic analysis, in “Topics in analysis and applications: selected theses”, R. Coifman, ed. World Scientific Publishing, 2000.
  3. T. Tao, Analysis I, Hindustan Book Agency, 2006.
  4. T. Tao, Analysis II, Hindustan Book Agency, 2006.
  5. T. Tao, Solving mathematical problems, second edition, Oxford University Press, 2006.
  6. T. Tao, V. Vu, Additive Combinatorics, Cambridge University Press, 2006.
  7. T. Tao, Nonlinear dispersive equations: local and global analysis, CBMS regional series in mathematics, 2006.
  8. T. Tao, Structure and Randomness: pages from year one of a mathematical blog, American Mathematical Society, 2008.
  9. T. Tao, Poincaré’s legacies: pages from year two of a mathematical blog, American Mathematical Society, 2009.  (Includes graduate texts on ergodic theory and on the proof of the Poincaré conjecture.)
  10. T. Tao, An epsilon of room, Vol I., Graduate Studies in Mathematics, 117, American Mathematical Society, 2010; and An epsilon of room, Vol II., American Mathematical Society, 2010.  (Includes a graduate text on real analysis.)
  11. T. Tao, An introduction to measure theory, Graduate Studies in Mathematics, 126, American Mathematical Society, 2011.
  12. T. Tao, Topics in random matrix theory, American Mathematical Society, 2012.
  13. T. Tao, Higher order Fourier analysis, Graduate Studies in Mathematics, 142, American Mathematical Society, 2012.
  14. T. Tao, Compactness and contradiction, American Mathematical Society, 2013.
  15. T. Tao, Hilbert’s fifth problem and related topics, American Mathematical Society. 2014.
  16. T. Tao, Spending symmetry, in preparation.
  17. T. Tao, Expansion in finite simple groups of Lie type, Graduate Studies in Mathematics Vol. 164, American Mathematical Society, 2015.
  18. T. Klowden, T. Tao, Climbing the cosmic distance ladder, in preparation.
  19. T. Tao, Six Math Essentials, Quanta Books, to appear, Nov 2026.

I will unfortunately not be able to respond to any requests for full-length copies of these books, beyond the material already linked to in these pages.

  3. Komplexität und Universalität, Terence Tao and Jochem Berlemann, Die weltweit besten mathematischen Artikel im 21. Jahrhundert, Band 2, 2014.  (A translated and illustrated version of this talk of mine.)

    The definition of addition is recursive, and relies on Proposition 2.1.16, which in turn uses pretty much all of the Peano axioms.       Reply

        5 September, 2025 at 7:36 am

        Terence Tao

        I was drawing a (rather subtle and technical) distinction between the external notion of a function in the ambient metamathematics (e.g., the metamathematics required to talk about a model of Peano arithmetic), and the internal notion of a function in a theory that itself has a notion of sets (and functions, etc.), such as ZFC. In Chapter 2 of this text, where Peano arithmetic is discussed, there is no internal notion of a set or a function yet (this is introduced in Chapter 3), so operations such as ++ are only functions in the external sense, not the internal one. However, once enough set theory is introduced internally in the model, one can also view these operations as internal functions as well.

        This distinction is however mostly of relevance when doing model theory (particularly when considering “nonstandard” models of various theories, such as nonstandard arithmetic), and can be safely ignored by the “working mathematician”.

        If you are interested in precise logical foundations for the material covered in this book, you can work through the Lean companion for the text, which formalizes all of this material in the proof assistant language Lean.

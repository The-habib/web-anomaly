Celebrating 250 years of discovery, creativity, and enterprise

Honoring the past. Celebrating American ingenuity. Inspiring the future.

See our upcoming events

 

Image

Honoring the past   Celebrating American ingenuity  Inspiring the future

In 1776, our nation’s founders declared independence based on three inalienable rights: life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. Over the past 250 years, innovators from coast to coast have helped turn those ideals into reality. Their ingenuity made our world safer, advanced our technological progress, and created prosperity for both the country and their families.  

As America’s Innovation Agency, the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) protects the inventions and brands that drive our economy forward. Join us as we explore the foundations of the intellectual property system in America, the history of patents and trademarks, and how innovation transforms our daily lives.

Since the first Patent Act was signed by President George Washington in 1790, the United States has granted more than 12 million patents and registered more than 8 million trademarks. During that time, additional foundational laws have modernized the intellectual property system and dedicated examiners have adopted new technologies to better serve applicants. 

From the lightbulb and the telephone to the brands we use every day, the USPTO remains a place where the past and future meet. Patents and trademarks tell a key part of America’s innovation story—from household conveniences to life-saving technologies and cultural icons. They protect inventors and creators, encourage investment in new ideas, and help turn a single idea into products, businesses, and public benefit.

Milestones in U.S. patenting

Throughout the nation’s history, inventors, lawmakers, and government employees have worked to expand and advance the patent system.

Image

From the first patents to today’s startups, American inventors and creators have transformed the world. The USPTO seeks to inspire and encourage innovation by highlighting innovators throughout history. Explore a few of their stories to learn about the importance of creating and protecting intellectual property, and the critical role it has played in innovations past and present.

Uncommon design, uncommon valor

The Medal of Honor, the nation’s highest military award, was designed by one of its recipients. Forty years after his service in the Civil War, he obtained a design patent to ensure the integrity of the medal.
George Gillespie

Bringing light for all

The inventor known best for improving a carbon filament for the incandescent light bulb used his childhood experiences, military training, and innovative mindset to fight prejudice and leave a lasting positive change in the world.
Lewis Latimer

A patent for girls’ empowerment

The founder of the Girl Scouts of the USA patented the organization’s iconic trefoil badge in 1914, which continues to signal girls’ invaluable contributions to American life and culture.
Juliette Gordon Low

Reframing entertainment

A high schooler made a puppet that landed him a television spot. Over the next 36 years, he used his knack for puppeteering, strong ability to collaborate, and unique sense of humor to create a cast of unforgettable characters.
Jim Henson

The patented boat that won the war

In the early hours of June 6, 1944, more than 135,000 Allied troops stormed the beaches of Normandy, France. The largest seaborne invasion in history could not have succeeded without the efforts of a shipbuilder from New Orleans.
Andrew Higgins

A girl’s window into history

Ignoring conventional wisdom on toys, an educator saw a market for tweens and created a beloved $300-million company. With more than 160 million books sold since 1986, her characters continue to help children explore American history.
Pleasant Rowland

Presidentially signed patents

Public Search Facility
USPTO headquarters

Patents were personally signed by the President of the United States from 1790 to 1836. Though many of these early patents were lost in a devastating fire, you can visit us to see surviving examples from our archives in person.

Celebrating 250: Commemorative patent and trademark signing ceremony

Thursday, July 23, 11 a.m. ET
U.S. Department of Commerce

Held at the Commerce Research Library, in the U.S. Department of Commerce—the former home of the USPTO for more than three decades—this event symbolized the milestones built on the vision, effort, and innovation of inventors and entrepreneurs throughout our nation’s history.

Read the remarks by USPTO Director Squires

USPTO America 250 Flagship Celebration

Wednesday, July 1

The USPTO celebrated 250 years of American innovation and spirit with events at our Alexandria, Virginia, headquarters and other USPTO campuses across the country. The celebration featured Alexandria Town Crier Dr. Benjamin Fiore-Walker, performances by the U.S. Army Fife & Drum Corps (Old Guard) and the USPTO Patenteers Chorus, and remarks by USPTO Director John A. Squires. After the live portion, employees enjoyed light refreshments, selfie stations, discussions with Thomas Jefferson, an artful coloring activity, a digital wish wall, museum tours, employee exhibits, and more.

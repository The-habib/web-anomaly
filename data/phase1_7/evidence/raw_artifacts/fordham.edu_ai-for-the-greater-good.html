AI for the Greater Good

We’re preparing our community—students, faculty, staff, and neighbors—to harness the power of artificial intelligence with wisdom, justice, and ethical discernment.

THOUGHT LEADERSHIP

Leading with our values

Experts at Fordham are studying the uses and effects of AI in the classroom and beyond. Many of our faculty are leading the conversation around using AI as a tool to serve human dignity and the greater good. Read more about their work and the insights they’ve offered the Fordham community:

Su-Je Cho, Professor of Curriculum and Teaching

I work with pre-service and in-service teachers on how to use AI equitably and responsibly in their classrooms.

In my courses, I use AI to model how to differentiate instruction for students with disabilities and English language learners—an essential skill that many find challenging. For example, I upload a lesson plan to ChatGPT, along with the learning profiles of students who have academic or behavioral difficulties, and ask for differentiated instructional strategies. I evaluate the AI-generated ideas and use examples to demonstrate how teachers can best tailor instruction.

Carolina Villegas Galaviz, Visiting Research Scholar, Law and Ethics

I examine the ethical implications of introducing AI into business.

Many companies are taking important steps toward ethical practice, and we’re eager to partner with them to translate ethical principles into effective, real-world applications. I'm optimistic. AI systems are complex processes that often involve many people, which makes it challenging for companies to fully grasp the ethical implications of their actions. But with thoughtful examination and critical thinking, it is absolutely possible.

Sam McGrath, Asst. Professor of Philosophy

I use AI to generate new philosophical insights into perennial questions about the nature of the mind and human language.

The increase in AI's ability to perform certain tasks will allow us to prioritize critical thinking, analysis, and contemplation of big questions, like ‘What are the things we value? ‘How do we want to live our lives? ' and ‘What do we want our societies to look like? The increasing power of AI makes those questions more salient and important to answer.

Navid Asgari, Assoc. Professor of Strategy and Technology Management

I’m interested in how AI affects organizations and organizational structures.

AI can be very useful, but it cannot do what humans do. Think about a programmer writing code. That’s a task. But a programmer in an organization does a variety of tasks that are related to one another in a very complex manner. You can replace every single one of these individual tasks that a person does with some sort of AI, but putting them together into a job? That’s a completely different challenge.

Giacomo Santangelo, Senior Lecturer of Economics

I’m interested in economic justice and the placement of AI infrastructure, such as data centers.

We did not have the opportunity to discuss economic justice during the Industrial Revolution, but we have that opportunity now with AI. When we’re building it, using it, and talking about it, we should be considering not just how it's going to be used to help the bottom line. We should be asking, ‘How is this going to maximize benefits to our neighbors and to ourselves, and to make our society better?”

Yilu Zhou, Assoc. Professor of Information, Technology, and Operations

My focus is on deep learning methodologies in AI to tap into business problems, such as working with designers to identify fashion trends based on runway data or determining whether an app is suitable for children.

In the past, you had to have a PhD to utilize natural language processing and image recognition to extract this kind of information. Now, with sweeping advancements in AI technology, everyone can perform information extraction. I teach my students how to do it.

AREAS OF STUDY

Prioritizing Career Readiness

We’re leaning into courses and training to ensure that our students are ready to apply AI in their jobs. As a rapidly growing tool, AI is becoming more prominent in everyday life. Our goal is for students to be prepared to apply AI to their field of study, gaining the  practical skills to responsibly use the tool in their careers.

SERVING NYC

Building a Community Hub at Fordham

We’re fostering collaborations with our neighbors in Manhattan and the Bronx to help provide education and access to AI. Fordham’s experts are committed to serving as educators for others and engaging with new insights around using AI responsibly. We’re eager to advance our understanding by learning from and with our community.

AI x Business Education

Set to debut in 2026, Fordham University's AI Hub in the Gabelli School of Business will serve as a collaborative center of innovation for industry, community, and higher education.

A platform to grow talent, commercialize solutions, and drive inclusive economic growth, the Hub serves a role that only Fordham can fill, turning ethical AI business innovation into real-world implementation.

  • Piloting AI solutions for industry partners that can be adopted at scale
  • Empowering entrepreneurs to work hand-in-hand with AI experts
  • Preparing the next generation of workers to lead AI application in their fields
  • Identifying best practices that NYS can share with industry, governmental, and partner networks
